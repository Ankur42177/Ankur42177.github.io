Between 2020/21 and 2023/24, Manchester City did something no English men's team had ever done: they won **four top-flight league titles in a row**. Pep Guardiola's system changed a lot over those four seasons. Strikers came and went, full-backs became midfielders, and centre-backs became full-backs. One thing stayed the same: **Kevin De Bruyne** decided the biggest moments.

## 2020/21: the false nine years

City began the season without a reliable striker. Sergio Agüero was often injured, and Pep frequently played with **no recognised No. 9**. De Bruyne became the answer in two ways:

- At times he played as a **false nine**, dropping into midfield to drag centre-backs out of position for runners like Gündoğan and Foden.
- In his usual right **"free eight"** role, he became the team's main source of chances.

City went from mid-table in December to champions by May. De Bruyne was named **PFA Players' Player of the Year**, the second year running.

> When the team had no focal point, De Bruyne *became* the focal point, as a creator rather than a finisher.

## 2021/22: the decisive goals

This was the title race against Jürgen Klopp's Liverpool that came down to a single point (93 to 92). De Bruyne added goals to his game: **15 in the Premier League**, his best scoring season. The standout was **four goals in the first hour at Wolves** in May 2022, in a 5–1 win right at the climax of the run-in.

He was voted **Premier League Player of the Season**, his second time winning the award.

## 2022/23: the Haaland connection

Erling Haaland's arrival could have reduced De Bruyne's influence. It did the opposite. A striker who constantly attacks the space behind defenders is the perfect target for De Bruyne's favourite pass: the whipped, low cross into the **"corridor of uncertainty"** between the defensive line and the goalkeeper.

De Bruyne finished with **16 Premier League assists, the most in the league**, while Haaland set the 38-game scoring record. It was the treble season, and the partnership was the engine of it.

## 2023/24: the comeback

On the opening night at Burnley, De Bruyne tore his **hamstring**, needed surgery and missed around **five months**. City stuttered without him, going through a winless spell in late autumn.

His return in January 2024 at Newcastle was like a film script. He came off the bench with City trailing 2–1, **scored the equaliser** and then **set up Oscar Bobb's late winner**. From there City went on an unbeaten run in the league and beat Arsenal to the title by two points on the final day.

## What made him irreplaceable

1. **Range of passing.** Through balls, switches and early crosses with either foot. Defences could never fully set up against him.
2. **Positional freedom.** Pep gave him licence to drift into the right half-space, where his crossing and shooting angles were most dangerous.
3. **Big-moment temperament.** Title deciders, comebacks, injury returns: he delivered in all of them.

Four titles, four different versions of City, and one constant. Pep's system set the structure, and De Bruyne was the one who turned it into goals.
