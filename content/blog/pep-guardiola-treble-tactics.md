On **10 June 2023** in Istanbul, Rodri's strike against Inter Milan completed something only one English men's team had managed before: the **Premier League, FA Cup and Champions League in a single season**. It was Pep Guardiola's first European title at City, and it came from a quiet but radical change in how his team was built.

## The problem Pep had to solve

For years City dominated the ball but were vulnerable to fast counter-attacks, especially in big Champions League nights. Signing **Erling Haaland** made things more complicated. A true No. 9 who stays high means one fewer player helping build attacks, so the structure behind him had to become more secure.

## The answer: a back four that becomes a 3-2-4-1

Out of possession City still looked like a 4-4-2 or 4-1-4-1. With the ball, everything changed:

- **John Stones** started at right-back or centre-back, then **stepped into midfield** next to **Rodri**.
- The other three defenders (often **Walker, Rúben Dias and Akanji**, or **Aké** on the left) held a **back three**.
- That created a **"box" midfield**: Stones and Rodri at the base, with **Kevin De Bruyne** and **İlkay Gündoğan** or **Bernardo Silva** ahead of them.
- **Grealish** and the right winger held the width, and **Haaland** pinned the centre-backs.

The result was a **3-2-4-1** in build-up. It gave City extra passing options in the middle and kept five players behind the ball as protection.

## Why four centre-backs mattered

From early 2023 Pep often used **four natural centre-backs** across the back. That trade-off was deliberate:

1. **Rest defence.** When City lost the ball, tall and strong defenders were already in place to stop the counter.
2. **Aerial security.** Fewer cheap goals from crosses and set pieces.
3. **Control over chaos.** City gave up some attacking width from the full-backs to get stability. With Haaland scoring, they didn't need to overload the box.

## Grealish: the controller, not the dribbler

Jack Grealish's role on the left was misunderstood. Pep didn't ask him to beat three players. He asked him to **hold the ball, draw fouls and slow the game down** so City could reset their shape. In a team built around control, a winger who almost never loses possession is a tactical weapon.

## Haaland: the focal point

Haaland scored **36 Premier League goals**, a record for a 38-game season. His presence changed how opponents defended. Centre-backs couldn't step out to press because Haaland would run in behind, so City's midfield had more time on the ball.

## The defining nights

- **Real Madrid 1–1, then City 4–0** in the semi-final, possibly the most complete Champions League performance by an English side. City's box midfield completely took over the game.
- **Inter 0–1 City** in the final was tense and imperfect, but the structure held. Rodri, the anchor of the whole system, scored the winner.

## What I take from it

The treble wasn't won by a new formation on paper. It was won by **controlling space in transition**: making sure that the moment City lost the ball, the right players were already in the right places. Guardiola has always said the best way to defend is to have the ball. In 2022/23 he also built a team that was ready for the moments it didn't.
